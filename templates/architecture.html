2. Mathematical & Algorithmic Formulation
Formal definitions implemented in models/ranker.py

A. TF-IDF (Term Frequency - Inverse Document Frequency)

Evaluates the relative importance of a keyword within the resume corpus, penalizing generic stop words while elevating rare technical terms.

TF(t, d) = f(t, d) / ∑ f(t', d)

IDF(t, D) = log((1 + |D|) / (1 + |{d ∈ D : t ∈ d}|)) + 1

B. Cosine Similarity in Vector Space

Measures the angular cosine distance between the Job Description vector u and Candidate Resume vector v, invariant to document length.

Cosine_Sim(u, v) = (u · v) / (||u||₂ · ||v||₂)

Semantic_Score = round(Cosine_Sim(u, v) * 100, 2)

C. Weighted Multi-Criterion Synthesis Formula

Prevents keyword stuffing by synthesizing four independent evaluation vectors with user-configurable weights:

Overall_Score = (W_skill × S_skill) + (W_sem × S_sem) + (W_exp × S_exp) + (W_edu × S_edu)
Default Weights: W_skill = 0.45, W_sem = 0.25, W_exp = 0.15, W_edu = 0.15
3. Frequently Asked College Viva Questions & Answers
Key questions professors ask during capstone project presentation
Q1: Why is Cosine Similarity preferred over Euclidean distance for text comparison?

Euclidean distance is sensitive to document length (a 4-page resume will have large magnitude vectors even if relevant). Cosine similarity measures the angle between vectors rather than Euclidean distance, making it length-invariant and focusing purely on term distribution and vocabulary alignment.

Q2: How does the system handle multi-word skills like 'Machine Learning' or 'REST API'?

The TF-IDF vectorizer is configured with an n-gram range of (1, 2) to capture both unigrams and bigrams. Concurrently, the entity extractor uses regex phrase boundaries against a structured taxonomy of known technical skills, preventing false positives (e.g., distinguishing 'C' from arbitrary words).

Q3: How does your system address "keyword stuffing" where applicants paste invisible skills?

Our system uses multi-factor weighting rather than single-metric scoring. Even if a candidate stuffs keywords, the experience alignment (15%) and education qualification (15%) check for verified work tenure and degrees, and sublinear term frequency scaling dampens repetition penalties.

Q4: What makes this an Explainable AI (XAI) system?

Unlike opaque neural networks that emit an arbitrary score, our engine produces transparent sub-scores (Skill Match %, Semantic %, Exp %, Edu %) and generates explicit bullet points identifying which required skills are matched, which are missing, and why a specific hiring verdict was recommended.
